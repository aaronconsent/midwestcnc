## The Three-Question Decision Tree

Every service call runs through the same logic. The answers tell us whether the job is on-site, bench, or a mix of both.

**Question 1: Does the repair need controlled metrology?**

Spindle rebuilds require balancing and runout checks below 0.0001 inches. That work fails on a production floor. Forklifts run nearby. Temps swing. Nearby machines add vibration. If the answer is yes, the part ships to us. Our [CNC Machine Repair Services](/repairs/) page lists the work we do. Spindle rebuilds fall squarely into the category where bench work requires controlled conditions.

**Question 2: Is the fault in a part you can remove?**

A dead spindle motor on a Mazak Integrex can be pulled and shipped. The machine stays put. A ballscrew on a Haas VF-2 can come out for inspection. But some faults need the full system. Alignment drift, way geometry errors, and control cabinet issues need live testing. We see this often on [Fanuc CNC Machine Repair](/repairs/fanuc-cnc-machine-repair/) jobs. The control and drives must run under load.

**Question 3: What is the total cost including downtime?**

This is where the math gets personal. Shipping a spindle from Des Moines to Waterloo runs under $200 each way. Shipping a VMC from Houston runs $3,000 to $5,000 with crating. If the repair is $4,000 and downtime is locked in either way, freight is the variable. But consider this: on-site work might get you running in one 8-hour visit. Shipping might mean 10 days transit each way plus bench time. Downtime cost may dwarf the travel bill.

These three questions form the core of our three-question decision tree. We can answer them in 10 minutes on a call.

## The Cost Framework for Shipping vs. On-Site

The cost framework for shipping looks at every line item, not just the obvious ones.

**Shipping costs for parts:**

- Spindles: $150 to $400 each way. Weight and routing matter.
- Motors and drives: $75 to $200 each way.
- Ballscrews: $100 to $300 each way. Custom crating is often needed.

**Shipping costs for whole machines:**

- Small VMCs under 10,000 lbs: $1,500 to $3,000 from the upper Midwest.
- Large horizontals or lathes: $3,000 to $6,000.
- Machines from Texas: add $1,500 to $2,500.

We have shipped machines from [CNC Repair & Service in Houston, Texas](/service-area/houston-texas/). We handle jobs in Missouri, Nebraska, and Wisconsin. Freight math shifts past the 500-mile mark.

**On-site costs:**

- Travel time billed portal-to-portal at our standard rate.
- Mileage or per diem for overnights.
- Bench time for any parts that still ship to Waterloo.

Shops within a 4-hour drive-time radius from Waterloo often save with on-site work. That holds for jobs that skip controlled metrology. Shops in Minneapolis, Chicago, or Kansas City fall closer to a toss-up. Shops in Texas or beyond usually save by shipping. The exception is pure diagnostics.

## Bench Work Handles Precision Assemblies

Some repairs cannot happen on-site. Spindle rebuilds are the clearest case.

A spindle arrives at our shop. We tear it down in a clean bay. We check every bearing seat and journal surface. We log runout at multiple points before any work starts. After rebuild, we balance the rotating assembly. We verify runout to 0.0001 inches or better. That takes granite plates, precision indicators, and stable temps. A production floor cannot match those conditions.

Precision grinding follows the same rule. We do that work in-house. A worn taper or scored bearing journal can often be saved. But only on the right gear.

We see this with [Mori Seiki CNC Machine Repair](/repairs/mori-seiki-cnc-machine-repair/) spindles. We handle [Makino CNC Machine Repair](/repairs/makino-cnc-machine-repair/) units. We rebuild [Toyoda CNC Machine Repair](/repairs/toyoda-cnc-machine-repair/) heads. The brand matters less than the physics. High-speed rotating parts need bench work in a controlled setting.

## On-Site Work Fits System-Level Repairs

On-site service is the right call when the machine must stay put. The job must not need metrology.

**Drive swaps:** A dead servo drive on a Fanuc-controlled machine can be diagnosed, sourced, and installed in one visit. The control stays live. The machine stays bolted down. We verify before we leave. This is core field work.

**Alignment and geometry:** If cuts are out of spec but the spindle tests fine, the fault may be alignment. That work has to happen on the installed machine. Ways and axes must be in their real-world state.

**Electrical diagnostics:** Intermittent faults, grounding issues, and control communication problems need live load testing. Shipping would not help. The fault would not show without the full install.

**ATC and hydraulic repairs:** Tool changer problems, hydraulic unit failures, and coolant system faults are almost always on-site. These systems tie into the machine install. Shipping rarely helps.

## The Hybrid Approach Cuts Downtime

Many jobs split between field and bench. A typical flow looks like this:

1. Tech arrives on-site to confirm the fault.
2. Tech pulls the failed part—spindle, motor, ballscrew.
3. Part ships to Waterloo for bench repair.
4. Tech returns to reinstall and verify.

This path cuts downtime. Bench work runs while your other machines run. It also keeps costs down. Field visits are short and focused.

The hybrid path is common for shops in the 200- to 400-mile range. Closer shops may want all on-site work. Travel time is minor. Shops past 500 miles may ship the whole machine. That makes sense if the repair is big enough to justify freight.

## Preparing for a Ship-In Repair

If shipping is the right call, a few steps speed things up.

**For parts:**

- Drain all oil and coolant before crating.
- Coat bearing surfaces, tapers, and journals with rust block.
- Send the hardware that came off during removal. The drawbar matters for spindles.
- Email us photos before you ship. We like to know what to expect.

**For whole machines:**

- Use a qualified millwright for rigging and crating.
- Lock the axes. Pull tooling from the spindle. Drain fluids.
- Note the install details: anchor points, power specs, coolant lines. That makes reinstall smooth.
