- OEM way covers ship in 4 to 8 weeks on current machines, while custom covers average 2 to 4 weeks from field measurement to delivery.
- Custom fabrication is the right answer in three eras: machines with discontinued OEM part numbers, orphaned platforms with no parts support, and machines with heavy customization history.
- A 1998 Mazak VTC-16B with modified Z-axis travel required custom covers at 35% lower cost than sourcing discontinued OEM panels.
- Field measurements take 4 to 6 hours and can be scheduled within 48 hours across Iowa, Illinois, Wisconsin, and Minnesota.
- The break-even point falls at machines 12 to 15 years old, where OEM pricing grows unpredictable.

When a way cover fails, the shop faces a choice. OEM replacement or custom fabrication? The answer sets the timeline and the final cost. OEM covers fit without question, but lead times stretch long on older machines. Custom covers need good field data, but they often arrive faster and cost less. The right path depends on three things: machine age, modification history, and whether the OEM still stocks parts for that platform.

## The lead-time delta between OEM and custom

OEM lead times vary by brand and machine age. Current-production machines get covers in 4 to 8 weeks. [Haas CNC Way Covers](/way-covers/haas-cnc-way-covers/) on VF-series machines often ship in 4 to 5 weeks. Haas builds in the U.S. and stocks common parts. [Mazak CNC Way Covers](/way-covers/mazak-cnc-way-covers/) on Integrex platforms run 6 to 8 weeks, sometimes longer.

The lead-time delta of 2 to 4 weeks favors custom on most legacy platforms. A 2006 DMG Mori DMC 64V might quote 10 to 14 weeks for OEM covers. A custom set from field measurements ships in 3 to 4 weeks. That delta grows on platforms where the OEM has ended parts support.

Several factors shape custom lead times:

- **Material stock**: 1018 cold-rolled steel ships from Midwest distributors in 3 to 5 days. Stainless adds 1 to 2 weeks.
- **Cover style**: Bellows covers take less shop time than telescoping covers with wipers and overlap geometry.
- **Geometry**: A flat X-axis cover measures faster than a Y-axis cover with compound curves.

On new machines with strong OEM support, custom offers no time advantage. A 2022 [Haas Next Generation Control (NGC)](/way-covers/haas-cnc-way-covers/haas-ngc/) machine gets OEM covers faster through the dealer. Custom makes sense when OEM quotes stretch past 6 weeks or when pricing runs 2 to 3 times the fabrication cost.

## Three eras where custom is the right answer

Not every machine suits custom covers. But three situations point clearly toward fabrication.

### Era one: discontinued OEM part numbers

OEMs support parts for a window after production ends. Fanuc holds parts 15 to 20 years. Machine builders often hold parts 10 to 12 years, sometimes less on low-volume platforms.

When a cover part number shows discontinued, options narrow. The shop can hunt secondary channels, pay for a one-off OEM build, or go custom. Discontinued OEM part numbers almost always favor custom.

We have seen one-off OEM quotes run 3 to 4 times the custom price. Lead times hit 16 to 20 weeks. Secondary channels sometimes turn up NOS (new old stock) covers. But condition varies, and fit is unknown until the part arrives.

### Era two: orphaned machine platforms

Orphaned machine platforms have no OEM support at all. The builder was acquired, left the market, or ended all service. Examples include older Cincinnati Milacron verticals, DeVlieg jig mills, and certain Giddings & Lewis machines.

Custom fabrication is the only path for these platforms. No OEM exists to call. Secondary-market parts carry risk of mismatch from machine-to-machine variation. [Way Covers for Series 6 / 10 /](/way-covers/fanuc-cnc-way-covers/series-6-15-legacy/) 15 legacy Fanuc controls sometimes fall here when the machine builder has closed, even though Fanuc still supports the control.

Orphaned platforms need more careful measurement. No baseline OEM drawings exist. We add 1 to 2 hours and extra reference photos.

### Era three: heavy customization history

Machines with heavy customization history often no longer match original specs. OEM covers are wrong from the start.

Common mods that break OEM cover geometry:

- Extended or shortened axis travel
- Relocated coolant nozzles or chip conveyors
- Added pallet systems or loaders
- Non-standard fixturing on cover mounting rails

A 2003 Okuma LB3000 with a gantry loader added in 2011 could not use OEM X-axis covers. The loader brackets took 40mm of mounting rail. Custom covers cost 28% less than trying to modify OEM panels after delivery.

## Cost structure: OEM versus custom

Total installed cost goes beyond the quote. It includes the cover, freight, field measurement (for custom), and any fit-up work.

OEM pricing is stable on current machines. A Z-axis telescoping set for a new Mazak Variaxis i-700 runs $2,800 to $4,200. The same cover on a 2008 Variaxis 500-5X, if still offered, may quote $5,500 to $7,000 as a special order.

Custom covers price by material, labor, and complexity. A mid-size VMC X-axis set runs $1,800 to $3,200 in 16-gauge cold-rolled steel with standard wipers. Stainless adds 25% to 35%. Complex geometry with chip deflectors or unusual wipers can approach OEM pricing.

The break-even point falls at machines 12 to 15 years old. At that age, OEM pricing jumps and lead times stretch. Machines under 10 years with good support often favor OEM. Machines over 15 years almost always favor custom.

Freight also matters. [DMG Mori CNC Way Covers](/way-covers/dmg-mori-cnc-way-covers/) from Japan or Germany carry international shipping and customs delays. Custom covers from Iowa ship ground to our seven-state area in 2 to 4 days.

## Field measurement: the key to custom fit

Custom cover accuracy rests on field measurement. A 3mm error on a 1,200mm cover creates fit-up problems. We have seen shops attempt custom covers from old drawings or customer-supplied numbers. The failure rate runs 15% to 20%.

Our measurement process:

1. **Machine prep**: Power down, axes at mid-travel, old covers removed.
2. **Reference capture**: Mounting rail positions, travel lengths, clearance envelopes. We use calibrated instruments and laser distance tools.
3. **Interference mapping**: Coolant lines, chip conveyors, cable carriers, service panels.
4. **Photos**: High-resolution shots of every angle. These guide fabrication and remove ambiguity.
5. **Wiper spec**: We check wiper material condition and measure seal lip geometry.

Single-axis covers take 4 to 6 hours. Multi-axis sets on complex machines can run 8 to 10 hours. We schedule field measurements within 48 hours for Iowa machines. Illinois, Wisconsin, and Minnesota typically fall within 4 to 5 days.

[Okuma CNC Way Covers](/way-covers/okuma-cnc-way-covers/) need extra attention. Okuma's telescoping designs use overlap geometry that varies by series. [Way Covers for Mazatrol Smooth Era Machines](/way-covers/mazak-cnc-way-covers/smooth-control/) also require careful checks for generation-specific geometry.

## Material and style choices

Custom fabrication opens options that OEM sourcing does not. The right pick depends on environment, service life, and whether the shop wants to match original specs or upgrade.

### Material

**Cold-rolled steel (1018)** is the default. It is durable, easy to fabricate, and accepts paint or powder coat. Most OEMs use a carbon steel variant.

**Stainless (304 or 316)** fits high-corrosion shops: medical machining with harsh coolants, food-industry work, or facilities with poor coolant maintenance. Stainless adds 25% to 35% to material cost.

**Aluminum** rarely makes sense. It lacks steel's stiffness in thin gauges and wears faster under chip load.

### Style

**Telescoping covers** suit most VMCs and HMCs. They shed chips well and allow panel-by-panel replacement. [Fanuc CNC Way Covers](/way-covers/fanuc-cnc-way-covers/) on compact Robodrill-style machines often use telescoping designs with tight overlap.

**Bellows covers** work where space limits telescoping compression. They appear on older lathes and on machines with long travels. Bellows trap chips more than telescoping covers and need more frequent cleaning.

**Roll-up covers** fit niche cases where neither telescoping nor bellows geometry works. They are more common on grinders with very long travels.
